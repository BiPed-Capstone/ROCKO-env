# Battery voltage at 50 Hz, recorded and plotted beside the control effort

- **Date / commit:** 2026-11-27, built on `4439f23`
- **Branch:** `ai-forward`
- **Environment:** macOS laptop (host tests); dev container (Jazzy) for the build, the mock and the simulation.
  Nothing ran on the Pi.

## Goal and success criteria

The owner asked (2026-11-27): read the battery at 20 or 50 Hz instead of once a second, to see voltage brown-outs
(short sags while the motors work hard) during balancing, plotted with the control effort.

Checkable criteria:
1. `BatteryMonitor.py` reads the INA226 `sample_rate_hz` times a second (default 50), and publishes every reading,
   unsmoothed and stamped when it was read, on `/rocko/battery_raw`. The INA226 is set to make a new reading faster
   than that. (host `tools/extras_test.py`: the chip's conversion time is shorter than the sample period)
2. Everything that used the once-a-second reading still gets it: `/rocko/battery` (smoothed) and `/diagnostics` once a
   second; the supervisor's enable rule unchanged. (T2 `verify_supervisor.sh` section 12)
3. One failed or implausible reading skips that sample instead of dropping the sensor for 30 s (a brown-out may
   itself upset the I²C bus, which is when the data matters); a fault that lasts `battery.GIVE_UP_AFTER_S` is still
   reported and retried as before. (host `tools/extras_test.py`)
4. A recording (`record:=true`) holds `/rocko/battery_raw` and `/rocko/battery`. (host `tools/record_run_test.py`)
5. A Foxglove layout plots the raw battery voltage above the control effort and the pitch, on one time axis; every
   message path in it is a real field. (T3 `verify_tools.sh`, `check_foxglove_layout.py`)
6. The dashboard plots the lowest battery voltage in each of its 50 ms samples beside the wheel command, so a sag
   shorter than a sample still shows. (T3 `verify_tools.sh` dashboard check still passes; page looked at in a
   browser with made-up data)
7. Every tier that ran before still passes. (`tools/run_ladder.sh`)

## Plan

`battery.py`: a faster INA226 configuration (average of 4 instead of 16), the default rate, and a small
`BadReadings` helper for criterion 3. `BatteryMonitor.py`: two timers (sample, report). `RecordRun.py`: two topics.
`foxglove/rocko_battery_layout.json` and an `--optional-topic` for `check_foxglove_layout.py` (the simulation has
no battery monitor). `Dashboard.py` and `dashboard.html`: the battery plot. Docs: the extras page, the fitting guide,
record-a-run, Foxglove and dashboard guides, the ladder.

Risks: the battery bus is a software (bit-banged) I²C bus, and the kernel busy-waits while it clocks bits; 50 reads a
second may cost a few percent of one core, not measured. No §6 file changes.

## What changed

- **`battery.py`:** the INA226 now averages 4 conversions instead of 16 (`CONFIG_AVG4_CONTINUOUS`, a new reading
  every 8.8 ms, `READING_EVERY_S`), so a 50 Hz read always gets a fresh reading that covers under 10 ms of the
  battery; `DEFAULT_SAMPLE_RATE_HZ` 50, `MAX_SAMPLE_RATE_HZ` 100; `BadReadings` and `GIVE_UP_AFTER_S` (1 s); the
  INA226's die ID (`REG_DIE_ID`, `DIE_ID_INA226` 0x2260).
- **`BatteryMonitor.py`:** one timer reads at `sample_rate_hz` and publishes every reading on `/rocko/battery_raw`
  (stamped at the read, unsmoothed, never an implausible value); another, once a second, publishes the smoothed
  `/rocko/battery` and the health report, now with the measured rate and the skipped count. A second without one good
  reading reports WARN and publishes no smoothed voltage. It refuses a chip whose manufacturer or die ID is not the
  INA226's (asked for by the owner: "make sure the code is using the INA226 and not the 219").
- **`RecordRun.py`:** records `/rocko/battery_raw` and `/rocko/battery`.
- **`foxglove/rocko_battery_layout.json`:** battery voltage (by read time), control effort (pitch loop output,
  feedforward) and pitch on one time axis. `sim/check_foxglove_layout.py` gained `--optional-topic` and
  `verify_tools.sh` checks both layouts.
- **Dashboard:** the lowest battery reading in each 50 ms sample, as a tile and a plot beside the wheel command; a
  sample with no reading is a gap; the battery card shows readings per second and skipped readings.
- **`tools/battery_monitor_check.py`** (new, container, in `tools/run_ladder.sh`): the real node against a fake
  INA226.
- **Docs:** [the robot extras](../architecture/robot-extras.md#battery), [Record a run: look at a
  brown-out](../guides/record-a-run.md#look-at-a-brown-out), the fitting guide, the dashboard guide, the ladder, the
  glossary (Brown-out), next-steps row 4.3.

## What a human should do on the robot

After [fitting the battery monitor](../guides/fit-the-extras.md): check the dashboard's battery card shows about 50
readings/s and few skipped readings with the robot standing still; measure the loop timing with and without
`battery:=false` (the guide's CPU step); then, tethered, balance with `record:=true` and look at the run with
`foxglove/rocko_battery_layout.json` ([Record a run: look at a brown-out](../guides/record-a-run.md#look-at-a-brown-out)).
Nothing here says how deep the sags are or whether they matter: that is what the recording is for.
